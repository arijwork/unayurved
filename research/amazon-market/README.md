# Amazon market analysis

What the Amazon.in market for each product really is, after removing duplicates and products that are not ours to compete with.

## Files

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `raw/*.csv` | Helium 10 exports, one per search (exported November 2026). Revenue and units are Helium 10's monthly estimates. |
| `classify.py` | De-duplicates and sorts every listing into a bucket. |
| `cleaned-products.csv` | The result: one row per unique product per file, with bucket, reason, pack size, price, units and revenue. |

`data.json` and `products.json` are regenerated by the script and not committed.

## Rerunning with fresh exports

Replace the files in `raw/` (same file names), then:

```bash
python3 classify.py raw data.json
```

Add `--review` to print every assignment for checking.

## How listings are sorted

- **Duplicates.** The same ASIN twice in one file is a sponsored and an organic placement of one listing; it is counted once. Different pack sizes of a product are separate listings and all stay. Revenue is the per-listing figure (`ASIN Revenue`), not `Parent Level Revenue`, which repeats a product family's total on every variant.
- **Core.** The pure, single-ingredient product for personal or food use. Olive oil covers pomace, extra light and extra virgin cooking oil. Rose water includes edible gulab jal.
- **Adjacent.** The same ingredient in another form: blends, capsules, face wash, juice, cooking spray, toners with added actives.
- **Wrong audience.** The right ingredient for another use: neem oil for plants, puja and lamp oils, pet products.
- **Wrong product.** Does not contain the searched ingredient, or is one of the other six products appearing in the wrong file (then it is counted once, in its own market).

Sorting is by keyword rules on the title and Amazon category. The top sellers in each file were read by hand; low-revenue listings were not individually checked.
